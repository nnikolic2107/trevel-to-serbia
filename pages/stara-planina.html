Stara Planina (Balkan Mountain): The roof of the Balkans

If Kopaonik is the modern, high-energy "ski capital," Stara Planina is its wild, untamed sister. This mountain range, also known as the Balkan Mountains, is so significant that it gave the entire Balkan Peninsula its name. It is a place of untouched wilderness, where jagged peaks meet lush pastures and some of the country’s most spectacular waterfalls. Situated along the border between Serbia and Bulgaria, around 90km from Niš, Stara Planina is part of a massive 600-kilometer mountain chain. In Serbia, it stretches across the municipalities of Pirot, Knjaževac, Zaječar, and Dimitrovgrad. The mountain is home to Midžor, the highest peak in Central Serbia, standing at an impressive 2,169 meters. Reaching the summit of Midžor is a favorite for hikers, as it offers a unique "one foot in each country" experience along the border. Another iconic landmark is Babin Zub (1,758 m), famous for its "Grandmother’s Tooth" rock formation and serving as the primary hub for tourism and skiing.

Best Time to Visit Stara Planina

Stara Planina is a year-round destination, with each season offering a different experience. The best time to visit depends on your interests:

  • Winter (December to March): Ideal for skiing and snowboarding. The mountain is covered in snow for nearly five months a year and features one of Serbia’s most modern ski resorts, including the country’s first gondola. The center of gravity is Babin Zub and the Jabučko Ravnište plateau. For those who don't ski, the ridge towards Midžor is a prime spot for snowshoeing. The frozen waterfalls (especially Tupavica) become spectacular wall-sized ice sculptures.
  • Spring and Summer (April to September): The best time for hiking, mountain biking, and exploring waterfalls. August is particularly popular for trekking, though hikers should be prepared for the strong mountain sun. Summer is the only safe time for most tourists to summit Midžor (2,169 m). The trail is a long but rewarding ridge walk with 360-degree views into Bulgaria.
  • Autumn (October and November): Perfect for those seeking tranquility and vibrant "golden hour" landscapes as the dense beech forests change color. The air is crisp and clear, providing the best visibility for long-distance photography. The "heavy" heat of August is gone, making the steep climbs to waterfalls much more comfortable.

Top Activities and Attractions

  • Chasing Waterfalls: Stara Planina holds the highest concentration of waterfalls in Serbia. Tupavica Waterfall, often cited as the most beautiful in Serbia, this 15-meter fall looks like a natural lace curtain draped over red rocks. It is most spectacular in spring or after heavy rain, but in winter, it frequently freezes into a stunning "ice sculpture. Bigar Waterfall, located right by the main road toward Knjaževac, this is the most accessible "gateway" waterfall. It cascades over moss-covered tufa stone, creating a series of emerald-green pools that look like something out of a fairy tale. Dojkinačka River and the Arbinje region are widely considered the most beautiful in Serbia. Walking along the river, you’ll encounter a seemingly endless series of smaller cascades and rapids framed by dark green spruce trees and bright red riverbeds. The best time to photograph these waterfalls is late May and early June, when the snowmelt from the peaks turns these streams into roaring torrents.
  • Adventure Sports: Beyond standard hiking, the mountain is a hotspot for paragliding, mountain biking, and off-road quad biking.
  • Wildlife Watching: The mountain’s rich biodiversity includes bears, wolves, and over 200 bird species. Guided tours can help you spot these elusive creatures.
  • Gastronomy: No trip is complete without trying local specialties like Belmuž (a traditional cheese and cornmeal dish), Pirot’s famous ironed sausage (peglana kobasica), and local mountain honey.
