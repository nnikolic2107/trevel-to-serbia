General Entry Requirements for the Republic of Serbia

When entering Serbia, all travelers must comply with certain conditions, regardless of whether a visa is required.

To be granted entry, visitors must have:

  • A valid travel document (passport), or a valid identity card where permitted under bilateral agreements or government decisions;
  • A valid visa, if required for their nationality;
  • Sufficient financial means to cover their stay. This is generally considered to be at least €50 per day, which may be demonstrated through cash, bank statements, or other financial proof;
  • A health-related certificate, such as proof of vaccination or confirmation that the traveler is not carrying a contagious disease when arriving from affected areas, in accordance with Ministry of Health guidelines.

For minors:

  • Foreign minors are not required to have parental consent to enter Serbia;
  • However, Serbian citizens under the age of 16 traveling alone or with someone other than a parent or legal guardian must carry certified consent from their parents or legal representative.

It is also strongly recommended that visitors obtain health insurance valid for the duration of their stay, with coverage of at least €20,000 for potential medical expenses.

Reasons for Refusal of Entry

Under Serbian law, a foreign national may be denied entry if:

  • They do not possess a valid travel document or required visa;
  • They cannot demonstrate sufficient financial resources for their stay, onward travel, or return;
  • They are in transit but do not meet the entry requirements of the next destination country;
  • They are subject to a ban on entry, expulsion order, or other legal restriction;
  • They fail to provide required health documentation, when applicable;
  • Their entry is considered a risk to public order or national security;
  • They have an international criminal record;
  • There are reasonable grounds to believe they may misuse their stated purpose of visit.

Additional Documents That May Be Requested

Border officials may ask travelers to present supporting documents, including:

  • A tourist voucher issued by a travel agency;
  • An invitation letter from a private individual or company;
  • A return or onward ticket;
  • Proof of hotel reservation or accommodation address;
  • Evidence of health insurance coverage for the duration of stay;
  • Any other documents necessary to confirm the purpose of travel.

Important Phone Numbers

While staying in Serbia, visitors can use the following emergency and essential contact numbers:

  • Police: 192
  • Fire Brigade: 193
  • Ambulance: 194
  • General Emergency: 112
  • Roadside Assistance (AMSS): 1987

These emergency numbers are available 24/7 and are free of charge when dialed within Serbia. Operators for the 112 emergency number may also provide assistance in foreign languages.

For non-emergency assistance, travelers can contact their country's embassy or consulate in Serbia for support and guidance.
