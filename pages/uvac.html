Uvac Lake and Canyon – A Natural Wonder in Southwest Serbia

The Uvac Special Nature Reserve is home to one of the most breathtaking landscapes in the Balkans, characterized by the dramatic, emerald-green Uvac River meanders that snake through steep limestone cliffs. Located in southwestern Serbia, Uvac Lake lies about 280 km from Belgrade, 320 km from Niš and a bit less than 70km from Zlatibor, making it a popular destination for weekend nature trips.

This protected area is famous for being the primary habitat of the rare Eurasian griffon vulture, a majestic bird of prey with a 3-meter wingspan and weight of nearly 10 kg. Once on the brink of extinction in Serbia (with only about 10 pairs left in the 1990s), successful conservation efforts have turned this reserve into one of the largest colonies in the Balkans, now home to over 500 individuals.

One of the most famous places to experience the beauty of the canyon is the Molitva Viewpoint, widely considered the best panoramic viewpoint in Serbia. From this elevated spot, visitors can see the iconic horseshoe-shaped meander of the Uvac River, which has become one of the most photographed landscapes in the country. The viewpoint is accessible by a short hike from a nearby parking area and offers breathtaking views, especially during sunrise or sunset when the light highlights the curves of the river and the surrounding cliffs.

Another impressive viewpoint in the area is Ravni Krš, which provides a different but equally stunning perspective of the Uvac meanders and the vast canyon landscape. Ravni Krš is slightly less crowded than Molitva and is perfect for visitors who want a quieter experience in nature. The viewpoint is reached via a scenic hiking trail and is popular among photographers looking for dramatic views of the canyon and the soaring griffon vultures that often glide above the cliffs.

Best time to visit Uvac

Besides visiting the viewpoints, travelers can explore the canyon by boat tours across Uvac Lake, kayak along the river, hike through untouched nature, or visit the famous Ušak Cave System, one of the longest cave systems in Serbia. The best time to visit these attractions is from May to September, when the weather is most favorable for outdoor activities.

The Ušak Cave System: An Underground Labyrinth

Uvac isn't just beautiful on the surface; it sits atop one of the most significant cave systems in Serbia. The Ušak Cave System is over 6 kilometers long and connects several underground chambers and tunnels.

  • Ušačka Cave: The largest part of the system, it has two entrances: one in the village of Gornje Lopiže and another directly in the Uvac riverbed.
  • Ice Cave (Ledena Pecina): This is the most famous section, accessible only by boat. It stays at a constant 8°C year-round. It is famous for its "jewelry"—magnificent stalactites, stalagmites, and crystal-clear underground pools.
  • Tubića and Baždarska Caves: These are smaller but equally fascinating karst formations nearby, often included in professional speleological tours.
  • Prehistoric Secrets: Archaeological finds in these caves, including fossils of Cave Bears, suggest that the area served as a refuge for Ice Age animals thousands of years ago.
