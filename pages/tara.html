Tara Mountain: The jewel of Western Serbia

Tara Mountain, often called the "Lungs of Serbia," is one of the country’s most beautiful and best-preserved natural destinations. Known for its dense forests, dramatic viewpoints, and rich wildlife, Tara is a perfect place for travelers looking to experience Serbia’s wild and authentic nature. Tara Mountain lies within Tara National Park, in western Serbia near the town of Bajina Bašta. The mountain stretches along the Drina River canyon, forming a natural border between Serbia and Bosnia and Herzegovina. Because of its remote and protected landscape, Tara is considered one of the greenest areas in the country. Tara Mountain is easily reachable by car from several major cities in Serbia: Belgrade 190km, Niš 300km, and Novi Sad 250km. The mountain is often visited together with nearby destinations like Zlatibor and the historic town of Višegrad.

Best Time to Visit Tara Mountain

Choosing the best time to visit Tara National Park depends on whether you are seeking high-intensity adventure, a quiet sanctuary, or the perfect photograph. While the mountain is a four-season destination, the "best" window is generally considered to be late spring (May–June) and early autumn (September–October), when the weather is most stable for exploring its famous viewpoints and dense forests.

  • Spring (April–June): Spring is arguably the most dynamic time to visit. As the snow melts, the mountain’s water features—like the Vrelo River (the shortest river in Serbia) and the waterfalls—reach their peak flow. By May, the meadows are carpeted in wildflowers, and the air is incredibly crisp. While May is statistically the wettest month, the rain usually comes in short, refreshing bursts that leave the forests smelling of pine and damp earth. This is the ideal time for birdwatching and seeing the rare Pančić Spruce (Serbian Spruce) in its most vibrant green.
  • Summer (July–August): When the Serbian lowlands and cities swelter in 35°C+ heat, Tara remains a cool, breezy sanctuary with average daytime highs around 24°C–27°C. Summer is the peak season for mountain adventure; you can feel the spray of the Drina River while joining the famous Drina Regatta in July—a massive, festive boat parade that navigates the emerald waters below the mountain. Away from the water, the season is perfect for exploring the deep shade of the centuries-old forests or embarking on high-altitude trekking to the most remote viewpoints. It is also the best time for guided bear-watching tours, as the long daylight hours and dry, accessible trails allow you to venture deep into the protected habitats of the brown bear.
  • Autumn (September–October): Many locals consider autumn the "Golden Season." The vast deciduous forests of Tara transform into a mosaic of fiery reds, deep oranges, and brilliant golds. The weather in September is often "Velvet Season"—warm, stable, and less crowded than summer. The clarity of the air in October makes the views from the Banjska Stena lookout particularly sharp, offering unobstructed panoramas of the Drina Canyon stretching into Bosnia and Herzegovina. It is also the harvest season, perfect for tasting local honey and rakija (brandy) in nearby villages.
  • Winter (December–February): Winter turns Tara into a serene, slow-paced wonderland. While it isn’t a major commercial ski hub like Kopaonik, it is perfect for those who prefer cross-country skiing, snowshoeing, or "winter walks" through silent, snow-heavy pines. The mountain resorts offer a cozy, traditional atmosphere where you can warm up by a fireplace with hearty Serbian mountain food like komplet lepinja. Be aware that some remote roads can be challenging in heavy snow, so a 4x4 is often recommended for winter explorers.

Top Things to Visit & Do

  • Banjska Stena Viewpoint: This is the "crown jewel" of Serbian viewpoints. After a gentle walk through a pine forest, the trees open up to reveal the massive Drina River Canyon. At nearly 1,000 meters above sea level, you’ll look down at the emerald water winding between the mountains. It is the best spot for photography, especially during the "golden hour" just before sunset.
  • Zaovine Lake: A high-altitude artificial lake known for its crystal-clear turquoise water. It’s a peaceful spot for swimming, kayaking, or simply driving around the scenic bays.
  • The "House on the Rock" (Drina River House): Located at the entrance to Bajina Bašta, this world-famous tiny wooden house sits perched on a rock in the middle of the river.
  • Drina River Kayaking: Trade the hiking boots for a life vest! Kayaking on the Drina is an unforgettable way to experience the border between Serbia and Bosnia. The water is crystal clear and refreshing (around 10-12 degrees of Celzius). You’ll paddle past hidden waterfalls and limestone cliffs that are only visible from the water.
  • Perucac Lake and the "Year River" Vrelo: Nestled within the breathtaking landscapes of western Serbia, Perućac Lake and the nearby Vrelo River are two of the most fascinating natural attractions in the area of Tara National Park. Together they create a unique destination where pristine nature, crystal-clear water, and dramatic scenery attract visitors from all over the world
